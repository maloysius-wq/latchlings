# Latchlings Story Bible

## One-sentence premise

**Latchlings** is a cozy route-puzzle game about small creatures living ordinary lives across slowly drifting floating islands, and the Waykeeper who helps their paths keep working as the old Skyway network slips out of alignment.

The world is not ending. Nobody is trying to conquer it. The problem is that the world moves, everyday life still needs doing, and a good route today may not be a good route tomorrow.

The story should make every puzzle feel like a tiny lived-in problem rather than an abstract board.

---

## The world

### The Latchlands

The **Latchlands** are a loose archipelago of small floating islands. Some hold one cottage and a garden. Others hold markets, workshops, transit junctions, old civic buildings, groves, caverns, or entire little neighborhoods.

The islands naturally drift. This is not a disaster or a curse. Drift is simply part of the world.

Latchlings have always adapted to it.

### The Skyway

Generations ago, **Waykeepers** built and tended the **Skyway**, a flexible network of routes, anchors, gates, rails, turners, switches, doors, and other bits of infrastructure that let Latchlings travel safely as the islands shifted around one another.

Most modern Latchlings know how to *use* the Skyway. Very few understand the whole system.

For a long time that did not matter. The network quietly compensated for ordinary drift.

Now portions of it are slipping out of alignment faster than local communities can compensate.

The consequences begin small:

- breakfast deliveries take the long way around;
- a child cannot reach a favorite play spot;
- neighbors miss each other because a crossing no longer lines up;
- gardens receive the wrong carts;
- market lanes jam;
- old anchors no longer hold the intended route;
- entire islands gradually become inconvenient to reach.

That is where the player enters.

---

## The player: the Waykeeper

The player is **the Waykeeper**.

The Waykeeper is deliberately not given a fixed appearance, age, gender, or biography. The player occupies the role directly.

A Waykeeper sees the route logic other Latchlings usually take for granted. The puzzle board is the Waykeeper's way of understanding a real navigation problem in the world.

The player is not commanding the Latchlings as pieces. They are helping everyone understand the sequence of moves that will get the right people to the right places.

### Featured residents and helper crews

A named resident in a Story Card is the person whose errand, discovery, concern, or request gives that route problem its meaning. The puzzle board is not a literal cast portrait of that scene. The Latchlings visible on the board are the **helper crew** or **work party** pitching in with the Waykeeper to carry out the route.

That means a Story Card can be about Pippa while the active board contains Latchlings with different body colors and suit marks. Those board identities belong to the crew participating in that route problem; they do not need to visually match the featured resident. A featured resident may also be helping in-world, but the board remains a practical route abstraction rather than a one-to-one depiction of named characters.

The simple player-facing rule is: **When one Latchling needs help, the whole island pitches in.**

### Gameplay fiction

A board represents a practical route problem.

- **Board edges / rocks** are ordinary physical stopping points.
- **Other Latchlings** can become temporary route stops because people and carts occupy space.
- **Anchors** are deliberate Skyway stopping points.
- **Suit gates** are old civic routing marks that recognize a Latchling's black suit emblem.
- **Color gates** are later routing systems that recognize body color.
- **Rails** are directional Skyway channels.
- **Turners** redirect a continuous route without ending the trip.
- **Switches and doors** are active pieces of infrastructure whose usefulness depends on timing.
- **Nests** represent the destination where that Latchling's trip is complete.

The mechanics should never be explained as magic if a practical piece of tiny-world infrastructure can explain them instead.

## Tone rules

### What Latchlings should feel like

- Warm, cozy, observant, lightly funny.
- Small problems treated with sincere importance.
- A world that feels inhabited even when nobody is speaking.
- Story delivered in tiny bites: one-line situations, short milestone beats, environmental changes, and occasional larger chapter moments.
- Emotional stakes can grow without turning the game grim.
- The final feeling should be community, adaptation, and belonging.

### What Latchlings should avoid

- A conventional evil villain.
- Chosen-one prophecy language.
- World-ending purple-sky escalation.
- Long mandatory dialogue scenes between ordinary puzzle levels.
- Treating the Latchlings as helpless. They are capable people asking for specialized help.
- Explaining every decorative detail with lore.

### Comedy

Humor comes from disproportionate seriousness around tiny logistical problems.

Examples:

- a route crisis caused by a pie cooling on the wrong island;
- two children confidently creating a problem they absolutely cannot solve themselves;
- an adult insisting a parcel route is "perfectly efficient" immediately before it becomes a seven-stop disaster;
- a market lane backed up because everyone tried the same shortcut.

The joke should usually be affectionate rather than sarcastic.

## Little Home: the recurring household

The approved production title island is a real place in the story: **Little Home**.

It is the player's emotional anchor and the recurring home base for the campaign. The title screen shows five residents going about ordinary life while the larger story unfolds around them.

The household is a chosen-family style home. Their exact biological relationships do not need to be defined. They live together, care for one another, and share the work of keeping a tiny floating island running.

### Pippa

- **Visual:** lavender adult, club mark.
- **Role:** gardener and household organizer.
- **Title-screen routine:** tends the flowers and garden area.
- **Personality:** practical, patient, quietly stubborn about doing things properly.
- **Story function:** notices small changes first because she pays attention to routine. If the watering route takes four minutes longer than yesterday, Pippa knows.
- **Voice:** concise, warm, occasionally dry.

### Bramble

- **Visual:** coral adult, diamond mark.
- **Role:** courier, errand-runner, and enthusiastic fixer.
- **Title-screen routine:** moves parcels between the cottage and yard.
- **Personality:** curious, optimistic, always convinced there is a clever shortcut.
- **Story function:** naturally carries the story outward to neighbors, markets, workshops, and distant islands.
- **Voice:** energetic and solution-oriented, even when the solution is the reason the situation got complicated.

### Rowan

- **Visual:** mint adult, heart mark.
- **Role:** tree-tender and island caretaker.
- **Title-screen routine:** checks the tree and the edge of the island.
- **Personality:** observant, patient, thoughtful.
- **Story function:** understands the island itself. Rowan is the first household member to suspect the unusual route problems are connected to a larger change in the drift.
- **Voice:** calm and economical.

### Pip

- **Visual:** blue child, spade mark.
- **Role:** professional explorer, according to Pip.
- **Title-screen routine:** plays through the yard with Tansy.
- **Personality:** bold, competitive, inventive, allergic to the concept of an unnecessary detour.
- **Story function:** creates small route problems, discovers things adults overlook, and makes unfamiliar places feel exciting rather than threatening.
- **Voice:** decisive and wonderfully overconfident.

### Tansy

- **Visual:** coral child, heart mark.
- **Role:** co-conspirator, collector, and dramatic commentator.
- **Title-screen routine:** plays through the yard with Pip.
- **Personality:** expressive, imaginative, surprisingly perceptive.
- **Story function:** often gives emotional meaning to changes adults initially treat as logistics. A distant island is not merely farther away; it is where a friend lives.
- **Voice:** vivid, sincere, occasionally theatrical.

## Narrative structure

The campaign remains **400 verified puzzle boards**, organized as **8 chapters of 50 levels**.

Each chapter contains five informal story movements of ten levels each:

1. **Levels 1–10:** establish the chapter's everyday problem and new gameplay language.
2. **Levels 11–20:** widen the problem beyond Little Home.
3. **Levels 21–30:** reveal a complication or new piece of information.
4. **Levels 31–40:** connect the local problem to the larger Skyway mystery.
5. **Levels 41–50:** resolve the chapter and change Little Home in a small visible way.

Normal levels should use a compact title plus one sentence of context. Levels 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 may carry a slightly larger story beat in the completion UI.

No story beat should make the player wait through a long scene to reach the next puzzle.

---

# Chapter 1 — Morning Routes

**Location:** Sunpetal Meadows  
**Gameplay foundation:** board edges, rocks, continuous snapping  
**Story job:** establish Little Home, the Waykeeper, and the idea that ordinary routes have begun behaving differently.

Morning begins with tiny inconveniences. A breakfast basket takes the wrong crossing. Pippa's watering route no longer lines up cleanly. Pip and Tansy cannot reach a play spot using yesterday's path. Bramble blames "a particularly opinionated breeze."

At first the problems seem unrelated.

By the end of the chapter, Rowan has compared enough routes to establish that Little Home has shifted farther than normal overnight.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** The household realizes the bad routes are not individual mistakes.
- **20:** Neighboring meadow islands report the same problem.
- **30:** Old route markers are found slightly out of alignment.
- **40:** The Waykeeper successfully builds a reliable temporary morning circuit.
- **50:** The morning is finally running again, but Rowan confirms the drift has not stopped.

### Little Home reward

A **mailbox / route post** appears on Little Home, marking the first dependable route the Waykeeper restores.

---

# Chapter 2 — Neighbors

**Location:** Lanternwood Grove  
**Gameplay foundation:** other Latchlings as movable stopping points  
**Story job:** widen the cast from one household to a community.

Bramble's attempts to check on nearby households reveal that Lanternwood has become a maze of almost-connected paths. The solution is not to travel alone. Neighbors become part of one another's routes, standing in the right place at the right time so somebody else can stop safely.

The chapter turns the core stopper mechanic into a social idea: sometimes the route works because another person is there.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** The first neighboring household reconnects with Little Home.
- **20:** Lanternwood begins coordinating shared travel windows.
- **30:** Pip and Tansy make friends on an island that nearly drifted out of easy reach.
- **40:** The Waykeeper realizes the old Skyway expected communities to cooperate rather than route independently.
- **50:** Lanternwood's neighborhood circuit is restored.

### Little Home reward

A small **neighbor pennant / visitor token** appears near the cottage to show that Little Home is no longer isolated.

---

# Chapter 3 — Holding Fast

**Location:** Lodestone Caverns  
**Gameplay foundation:** anchors  
**Story job:** reveal the first serious evidence that the Skyway itself needs attention.

The route markers lead beneath a cluster of islands into Lodestone Caverns, where the Skyway's old anchor system can still be reached.

Anchors were never meant to freeze the islands in place. They create dependable moments of alignment inside a moving system.

Several have gone untended for years.

The Waykeeper learns to use them while Rowan discovers that the unusual drift is exposing infrastructure nobody in the household knew existed.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** The first buried anchor is brought back online.
- **20:** Old Waykeeper markings prove the anchors were designed for constant repositioning.
- **30:** Bramble finds records showing that route maintenance used to be a regular community job.
- **40:** A cavern shift briefly threatens the newly restored line, forcing the household to adapt rather than simply repeat the old plan.
- **50:** The anchor network stabilizes the local cluster and points toward older civic routes farther out.

### Little Home reward

A **restored anchor** hangs beneath Little Home as both useful infrastructure and a visible reminder of the Waykeeper's work.

---

# Chapter 4 — Market Day

**Location:** Masquerade Keep  
**Gameplay foundation:** suit gates  
**Story job:** show a larger public community and turn identity-based mechanics into believable civic infrastructure.

Masquerade Keep hosts one of the Latchlands' biggest rotating markets. Its old lanes use black suit marks as routing credentials so different carts, workers, and residents can move through narrow districts without colliding.

On market day, the misaligned Skyway turns that elegant system into adorable logistical catastrophe.

Everyone still intends to have the market.

The Waykeeper's job is to make that possible.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** The outer market lanes reopen.
- **20:** Pippa's produce finally reaches the correct stall.
- **30:** Tansy discovers the suit marks are older than the market itself.
- **40:** Records inside the Keep show that the civic routes once synchronized with distant Skyway stations automatically.
- **50:** Market Day succeeds, and the community begins sharing old route records with the Waykeeper.

### Little Home reward

A strand of **market bunting** appears near the cottage, a souvenir from the first major community event the household helps save.

---

# Chapter 5 — The Long Drift

**Location:** Prism Gardens  
**Gameplay foundation:** color gates plus suit logic  
**Story job:** make the larger drift emotionally real.

The Prism Gardens are built around color-coded transit lanes layered over the older suit system. They are beautiful, precise, and currently becoming very difficult to navigate.

More importantly, the gardens sit high enough to see the wider Latchlands.

From there, the household can finally see what Rowan suspected: several island groups are slowly separating from the routes that have connected them for generations.

The problem is no longer merely inconvenient.

If nothing changes, some communities will become genuinely isolated.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** The first color route is restored.
- **20:** The household sees how far one neighboring island has drifted.
- **30:** Tansy worries about friends in Lanternwood; the abstract problem becomes personal.
- **40:** The Waykeeper realizes restoring the *old* map exactly will not work because the islands are already somewhere new.
- **50:** Prism Gardens reconnects using an adjusted route rather than its historical alignment.

### Little Home reward

A small **telescope** appears on Little Home so the household can watch neighboring islands and track the changing drift.

---

# Chapter 6 — Old Ways

**Location:** Copperline Junction  
**Gameplay foundation:** directional rails and turners  
**Story job:** uncover what Waykeepers actually understood about the Skyway.

Copperline Junction is an old transit hub full of rails, turners, maintenance channels, and half-forgotten Waykeeper records.

The household expects to find instructions for putting everything back where it used to be.

They find the opposite.

The original Skyway was built to change constantly. Old Waykeepers did not preserve one perfect map. They watched the drift and kept rewriting the routes.

At some point, later generations automated more and more of that work. Eventually everyone forgot the maintenance philosophy underneath the machines.

The current crisis is not that the islands are moving.

It is that the network stopped moving with them.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** Copperline's first rail loop is restored.
- **20:** Bramble finds a maintenance diagram that contains several mutually contradictory "correct" routes from different years.
- **30:** The contradiction becomes the revelation: all of them were correct for their moment.
- **40:** The Waykeeper deliberately builds a route that has never existed before.
- **50:** Copperline reconnects to the modern Latchlands using a new map.

### Little Home reward

A small **Waykeeper compass / route relic** appears beside the cottage.

---

# Chapter 7 — Coming Together

**Location:** Stormswitch Foundry  
**Gameplay foundation:** switches, doors, rails, identity gates  
**Story job:** turn restoration into a community project rather than a solo rescue.

Stormswitch Foundry contains the active control hardware needed to coordinate larger parts of the Skyway. Nothing there works in isolation. Opening one route may remove a useful stop somewhere else. Timing matters.

So does trust.

By now the Waykeeper is not solving the problem alone. Communities from the Meadows, Lanternwood, Lodestone, the Keep, Prism Gardens, and Copperline are sharing observations and moving in coordinated windows.

The chapter's switch logic mirrors the story: one route changes because somebody somewhere else acted at the right time.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** The first cross-region switch line comes online.
- **20:** Several communities coordinate the same travel window successfully.
- **30:** A failed synchronization becomes a funny but useful reminder that a flexible network needs communication, not perfection.
- **40:** The rebuilt system begins responding to current drift faster than the old automated routes did.
- **50:** The Latchlands establish a living Waykeeper network rather than relying on one forgotten machine.

### Little Home reward

A small **docking beam / arrival platform** appears at Little Home, ready for more frequent visitors.

---

# Chapter 8 — Homeward

**Location:** Aurora Crown  
**Gameplay foundation:** every campaign mechanic  
**Story job:** resolve the Skyway without stopping the natural drift.

Aurora Crown is where the oldest surviving Skyway lines overlap. Reaching it does not reveal a master switch that can force every island back into place.

There is no master switch.

That was never the point.

The final chapter asks the player to use everything learned across the campaign while communities throughout the Latchlands maintain their parts of the network.

The goal is not to restore yesterday's map.

The goal is to make a Skyway that can keep becoming tomorrow's map.

### Milestone beats

- **10:** The first Crown route accepts both old and newly built connections.
- **20:** Previously separated island groups appear on the same living route map again.
- **30:** Pip and Tansy realize visiting friends no longer requires planning around a failing crossing.
- **40:** Little Home becomes one node in a community-maintained Skyway rather than the center of a rescue effort.
- **50 / Level 400:** The Skyway lives again. The islands continue to drift. The routes move with them.

### Little Home reward

Distant **connected islands / route lights** become visible around Little Home. The title screen that began as one tiny household now quietly shows the larger community surrounding it.

---

## Ending

The ending should preserve the existing phrase **Skyway Restored**, but clarify what "restored" means.

The Waykeeper has not stopped the islands from moving.

Instead, Latchlings across the archipelago have relearned how to watch the drift, communicate, and maintain routes together.

The final emotional beat returns to Little Home.

Pippa tends the garden. Bramble has another parcel. Rowan checks the tree. Pip and Tansy are already turning some harmless object into tomorrow's route problem.

In the distance, other islands drift past, connected by the living Skyway.

Ordinary life continues.

That is the victory.

## Per-level writing rules

Each of the 400 levels receives:

1. a compact title;
2. one sentence describing the tiny practical problem;
3. normal mechanic guidance when appropriate.

Level context should not imply board geometry that is not actually present. Prefer motives and destinations over literal claims about exact board objects.

Good:

> **Breakfast Detour**  
> Pippa wants the herb basket home before the kettle gives up waiting.

Good:

> **The Lantern Roundabout**  
> A neighbor promised supper, and Bramble promised the route was shorter this way.

Avoid:

> Cross the red bridge and move the blue cart around three rocks.

unless those objects are guaranteed by authored level data.

---

## Story beat rules

- Normal level completion stays fast.
- Levels ending in 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 may show one extra short paragraph.
- Chapter 50 beats may be slightly larger and mention the new Little Home keepsake.
- A player can always continue immediately.
- Story text should never obscure the move result or star result.

---

## Naming / terminology

Use consistently:

- **Latchling / Latchlings** — the people.
- **Latchlands** — the floating-island archipelago.
- **Little Home** — the recurring household island.
- **Waykeeper** — the player's role and the historical route-tending profession.
- **Skyway** — the route infrastructure linking islands.
- **drift** — the normal movement of floating islands.

Avoid introducing additional proper nouns unless they genuinely help the player remember a place, person, or mechanic.

---

## Core theme

The story ultimately says:

**A good path is not one that never changes. It is one that can change with the people who need it.**

That idea should remain implicit in most of the game and become explicit only near the end.
